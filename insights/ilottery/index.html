01Who sells online

13 US jurisdictions sell eInstants online. The four biggest lotteries do not.

The jurisdictions that sell eInstants today accounted for a third of all US lottery sales in FY2024: $35.5 billion across every product. California, New York, Florida and Texas sold the same amount again, $35.5 billion, and none of them offers eInstants online.

02Growth

In Kentucky, Georgia and Virginia, eInstant sales roughly tripled in three years

From FY2022 to FY2025, eInstant sales grew 3.5 times in Kentucky, 2.8 times in Georgia and 2.6 times in Virginia. North Carolina, which launched in November 2023, sold $2.6 billion in its first full year. Pennsylvania and New Hampshire grew more slowly, and Michigan shrank.

03Payouts

A Swedish eInstant pays back 50%. A Pennsylvanian one pays back 89%.

Across the 27 lotteries that publish return to player (RTP) for at least five eInstants, the median game pays back between 50% and 92% of what is staked. All eight North American lotteries have a median of 78% or more. In Europe, only Italy has a median above 74%. For a studio, that means a different maths model in almost every market.

What it means: the gap is wider than the percentages suggest. When every win is staked again, the same budget buys about 4.5 times as many games in Pennsylvania as in Sweden.

04Studios

Three studios make 88% of the attributed eInstants in North America

IWG, NeoGames and Brightstar together account for 88% of live titles where the studio is known. In the Nordics, Fennica Gaming alone makes more than half. The picture is a count of games in the lobby, not of sales.

    05Launch curve

    Most eInstants sell best in their first month

    Michigan published sales for every eInstant from 2019 to 2021. Of 64 games launched in that window, 42 peaked in their launch month and 61 within two months. Three months in, the typical game sold 28% of its peak. After a year, 12%.

    What it means: a lobby lives on its release calendar. Most of a game's lifetime sales arrive early, so the cadence of new titles matters as much as the hit rate.

      08Method

      How the atlas is built

      Sources

      Lottery lobbies and game rules, read from each lottery's public website or feed. Annual reports, financial statements and public records, including freedom-of-information releases. Procurement records and press releases from lotteries and suppliers. Country shapes from Natural Earth, via world-atlas.

      Confidence grades

      • ARead from the lottery's own source and archived.
      • BOfficial figures via a compilation, or the supplier's own release.
      • CA single secondary source, such as trade press.
      • DDerived or estimated. Not used for any sales figure on this page.

      Limits

      The studio is identified for 76% of listings and RTP is published for 40%. Sales metrics differ between lotteries, so levels are not compared across states. Daily lobby scans began in October 2026.

      We read what lotteries publish. We do not log in, get around blocks or ignore robots.txt.
